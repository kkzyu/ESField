%  ABSTRACT

\begin{abstract}
A grand challenge in structure-based 3D molecular generation is achieving
\emph{zero-strain} targeting of specific hydration sites: attracting ligand
atoms to displace high-energy water molecules (HEWs, worth 0.5--2.0~kcal/mol
in binding free energy) without corrupting the internal conformational quality
that the generative model has learned from protein--ligand co-crystals.
Existing generators suffer from \textbf{water-blindness}---their training data
lack explicit water molecules, so they never learn to exploit HEW sites---while
existing inference-time guidance methods operate only on global molecular
properties, incapable of the site-type-level, topology-level control that
water displacement demands.

A natural intuition would be to enforce site occupancy by rigidly fixing
anchor-atom coordinates (``hard-fix'').  We show, via the Kinetic Path Energy
(KPE) diagnostic framework, that this intuitively appealing strategy is
physically catastrophic: each coordinate overwrite constitutes an instantaneous
teleport in the flow-matching ODE, injecting infinite-velocity spikes that
cause a \textbf{31,468-fold surge} in destructive kinetic energy (98.5\% of
total ODE energy).  This excess energy locks and tears internal conformational
degrees of freedom, producing severely strained molecules with degraded
Vina docking scores.

We propose \textbf{Kinematic Anchor Guidance}, a principled alternative rooted
in classical mechanics.  The key insight is that hydration-site attraction is
fundamentally a centre-of-mass (CoM) operation: the guidance displacement field
is orthogonally decomposed into internal conformational ($\vint$) and
rigid-body translational ($\vcom$) components, and guiding forces act
\emph{exclusively} on the CoM subspace ($\reals^3$).  This mathematically
guarantees \textbf{zero strain} on all bond lengths, angles, and torsions---the
molecule is softly pulled toward the target site as a rigid body, preserving
full internal flexibility for natural conformational relaxation.

Across 6 pharmacologically diverse PDBbind pockets on DrugFlow (3,200+
generated molecules with full Vina docking), plus cross-validation on
TargetDiff (a structurally distinct DDPM-based diffusion model), kinematic
anchoring delivers: (i)~\textbf{31,468$\times$ KPE suppression}
($\KPERatio=0.006\%$ vs.\ 98.5\% for hard-fix), providing ironclad physical
validation of the zero-strain guarantee; (ii)~\textbf{simultaneous improvement
in both occupancy and affinity}---the best Vina score in every pocket with
Wilcoxon signed-rank $p = 4 \times 10^{-6}$ over hard-fix; and
(iii)~\textbf{2$\times$ variance reduction} ($\sigma_{\text{Vina}}$: $0.52 \to
0.27$), eliminating the catastrophic conformational collapse (Vina $=-1.70$
kcal/mol) and repulsive outliers ($+6.86$ kcal/mol) that plague hard-fix and
annealing baselines.  These results establish that physically consistent,
zero-strain hydration-site targeting is both achievable and essential for the
next generation of water-aware molecular design.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Water as a First-Class Design Constraint}

Water molecules at protein--ligand interfaces are not passive bystanders.
Their thermodynamic signatures---whether a water is ``happy'' (structurally
conserved, mediating critical H-bond networks) or ``unhappy'' (high-energy,
trapped in a hydrophobic sub-pocket)---can be worth 0.5--2.0~kcal/mol per
molecule in binding free energy~\cite{dunitz1994,abel2008,michel2009}.
Displacing a single high-energy water (HEW) with a compatible ligand
functional group can therefore contribute binding affinity comparable to a
strong hydrogen bond~\cite{ladbury1996,spyrakis2017}.

Computational tools for predicting hydration thermodynamics are
well-established: WaterMap~\cite{abel2008}, Grid Inhomogeneous Solvation
Theory (GIST)~\cite{nguyen2012gist}, Grand Canonical Monte Carlo
(GCMC)~\cite{ross2012gcmc}, and 3D-RISM~\cite{luchko2010rism} each quantify
water energetics from molecular simulations or integral equation theory.
Yet these tools produce \emph{analyses}---heat maps and free energy
estimates---that medicinal chemists must manually interpret.  They have not
been integrated as explicit, differentiable design constraints within
\emph{de novo} 3D molecular generation pipelines.

\subsection{The Water-Blindness of Deep Generative Models}

Recent structure-based 3D molecular generators---TargetDiff~\cite{guan2023targetdiff},
DiffSBDD~\cite{schneuing2024diffsbdd}, DecompDiff~\cite{guan2023decompdiff},
DrugFlow~\cite{drugflow2024}, and FLAG~\cite{zhang2024flag}---have demonstrated
impressive capabilities: they generate drug-like molecules directly conditioned
on protein pocket geometry, achieving high validity rates and competitive
docking scores.  At their core, these models learn $p(\text{ligand} \mid
\text{pocket})$, a distribution over atom types and coordinates conditioned
on protein atoms.

A critical limitation lurks in the training data.  Crystallographic structures
from the Protein Data Bank~\cite{berman2000pdb} that comprise the training
corpora rarely retain explicit water molecules at the binding interface.
Consequently, the learned distribution $p(\text{ligand} \mid \text{pocket})$
encodes no information about \emph{which} pocket regions should be occupied
by ligand atoms versus water molecules.  The model has no incentive to
displace a HEW---it may equally well place a methyl group adjacent to the
water site, leaving the energetically unfavorable water in place and
forfeiting a significant binding affinity opportunity.  We term this
deficiency \textbf{water-blindness}.

Our earlier diagnostic work~\cite{esfield2026,esfield2026v6} established
the severity of this blindness.  Across 10 pharmacologically diverse PDBbind
pockets, a state-of-the-art DrugFlow generator achieved \textbf{0\% HEW site
occupancy} in unguided mode---not a single generated molecule placed any atom
within 2.5\,\AA{} of a HEW site.  This failure is not due to poor molecular
quality: the baseline molecules were chemically valid, drug-like
(QED $=0.39\pm0.14$), and achieved reasonable Vina scores.  Rather, the
generator's implicit geometric prior fundamentally diverges from the explicit
physicochemical objective of water displacement.

\subsection{The Naive Fix and Its Hidden Catastrophe: A KPE Diagnosis}

Faced with water-blindness, the most instinctively appealing strategy
is straightforward: during generation, \textit{identify atoms near HEW
sites, and rigidly fix their coordinates to enforce occupancy.}  This
``hard-fix'' approach---deterministically overwriting selected atom
coordinates to target values after each ODE integration step---has been
employed in various forms by fragment-fixing strategies in conditional
generation~\cite{griesbacher2026ebmol} and by the two-stage topology-aware
framework of \ESField~v7.1~\cite{esfield2026}.  The intuition is
compelling: if you want atoms at specific locations, simply force them
there.  What could go wrong?

To answer this question rigorously, we turn to the \textbf{Kinetic Path
Energy (KPE)} diagnostic framework recently formalized by Li et
al.~\cite{li2026kpe}.  KPE quantifies the transport cost of a flow-matching
trajectory by measuring the integrated squared norm of the effective
velocity field:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:kpe_def}
\KPE = \int_{0}^{1} \mathbb{E}_{\xt \sim p_t}\left[\|\bm{v}_\theta(\xt, t)\|^2\right] \, dt.
\end{equation}

Crucially, KPE decomposes into a \textbf{physiological component} (the
minimal kinetic energy of ODE transport following the learned data manifold)
and a \textbf{guidance deviation component} (excess energy injected by
external guidance signals).  A physically well-behaved guidance strategy
should contribute negligible energy relative to the ODE baseline.

Our KPE diagnosis of the hard-fix strategy reveals a devastating physical
picture.  Each time anchor coordinates are overwritten
($\bm{x}_{\text{anchor}} \leftarrow \bm{x}_0^{\text{anchor}}$), the
operation constitutes an \textbf{instantaneous teleport} in configuration
space:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:hardfix_delta}
\Deltax_{\text{hard-fix}}^{(i)} = \bm{x}_0^{\text{anchor},(i)} - \xt^{(i)},
\quad \forall i \in \mathcal{A},
\end{equation}

generating a velocity spike of effectively infinite magnitude
($\|\bm{v}\| \to \infty$) within a single integration step.
As quantified in Table~\ref{tab:kpe}, this injects \textbf{98.5\% excess
kinetic energy} into the trajectory---a 31,468-fold surge relative to the
physiological baseline.  The physical consequence is not merely numerical
artifact: the injected KPE directly \textbf{locks and tears internal
conformational degrees of freedom} ($\vint \approx \bm{0}$), preventing
the molecule from relaxing into a globally optimal binding pose.  The
result is the \textbf{Occupancy--Affinity Paradox}: molecules whose
anchors are successfully placed at HEW sites nonetheless exhibit
systematically \emph{worse} Vina docking scores than non-occupied
molecules (3mfw pocket: $p=0.067$, Cliff's $\delta=+0.47$, medium-to-large
effect size in the wrong direction).

Critically, this is not an implementation flaw that can be patched by
tuning hyperparameters.  The KPE catastrophe is a \textbf{fundamental
consequence of violating kinematic consistency} in the ODE: any
guidance strategy that overwrites coordinates without respecting the
underlying velocity-field structure will necessarily inject unbounded
kinetic energy.  Even annealing strategies that gradually release the
hard constraint after an initial fixation period still inject 69.5\%
excess energy (Table~\ref{tab:kpe}), because the irreversible KPE
spikes from the early hard-fix phase have already corrupted the
trajectory before release can begin.

The KPE diagnosis reveals a deeper structural insight that points
directly toward a solution.  Hard-fix injects energy indiscriminately
into all $3N_{\text{atoms}}$ degrees of freedom, yet the physical
objective---attracting a molecular fragment toward a point in
space---requires only the 3 degrees of freedom of rigid-body
translation.  This mismatch between the \emph{dimensionality of the
control objective} ($\reals^3$) and the \emph{dimensionality of the
control action} ($\reals^{3N_{\text{atoms}}}$) is the root cause of
the KPE catastrophe, and it suggests a general principle:
\textbf{constraint enforcement should operate in the smallest subspace
sufficient for the control objective.}  For hydration-site targeting,
this smallest subspace is the centre of mass (CoM).

\subsection{Kinematic Anchor Guidance: A Physically Consistent Solution}

We operationalize this principle through \textbf{Kinematic Anchor
Guidance}.  The approach has three conceptual steps (full mathematical
development in Section~\ref{sec:methods}):

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Decompose.} Separate the guidance displacement field into
    two orthogonal components: internal conformational motion ($\vint$,
    governing torsions, bond angles, and distances) and rigid-body
    centre-of-mass translation ($\vcom$).
  \item \textbf{Project.} Compute the gradient of the site-compatibility
    energy $E_{\text{site}}$ and project it onto the CoM subspace
    ($\reals^3$) only, discarding all components that would deform
    internal coordinates.
  \item \textbf{Attract, don't fix.} Apply this single CoM-directed
    force uniformly to all atoms under a time-annealed schedule
    $\lambda(t)$---strong during early topology-determining steps,
    decaying to zero during late geometric refinement.
\end{enumerate}

This procedure embeds a mathematically rigorous \textbf{Zero-Strain
Guarantee} (Theorem~1, Section~\ref{sec:methods}): because the guidance
displacement is identical for every atom, all internal coordinates (bond
lengths, angles, torsions) are provably invariant under the guidance
step.  The molecule is pulled toward the hydration site \emph{as a rigid
body}, with its full internal flexibility preserved for natural
conformational relaxation.

The philosophical contrast with hard-fix is instructive.  Hard-fix says:
``I demand this atom at this exact coordinate, regardless of physical
cost''---and pays a 31,468$\times$ KPE penalty.  Kinematic anchoring
says: ``I want the fragment to move toward this site, and I will gently
attract its centre of mass while letting every internal degree of freedom
breathe''---and incurs negligible energy cost ($\KPERatio = 0.006\%$).

\subsection{Contributions}

Our contributions span physical theory, algorithmic design, and
experimental validation:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{KPE diagnosis of hard constraint failure}: We provide the
    first quantitative physical explanation for \emph{why} naive coordinate
    fixation strategies degrade molecular quality in flow-matching
    generation.  The diagnosis reveals a 31,468$\times$ KPE surge
    (98.5\% contamination) as the mechanistic cause of conformational
    collapse---a finding with implications for all inference-time
    guidance methods that manipulate individual atom coordinates.
  \item \textbf{Kinematic Anchor Guidance}: We propose a principled,
    CoM-restricted soft guidance strategy grounded in velocity-field
    orthogonal decomposition.  It provides a mathematical zero-strain
    guarantee ($\vint \equiv \bm{0}$ modification) while enabling
    flexible, time-annealed attraction toward hydration sites---the
    first inference-time guidance method to offer site-type-level
    control with provable conformational preservation.
  \item \textbf{Comprehensive experimental validation}: Across 6
    pharmacologically diverse PDBbind pockets (3,200+ generated
    molecules, full Vina docking), kinematic anchoring achieves
    simultaneous improvement in HEW occupancy and binding affinity
    (Wilcoxon $p = 4 \times 10^{-6}$ over hard-fix), 31,468$\times$
    KPE suppression, and 2$\times$ variance reduction, establishing
    the first Pareto-dominant solution for zero-strain hydration-site
    targeting.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{3D Structure-Based Molecular Generation}

Structure-based \emph{de novo} molecular generation has been transformed by
deep generative models operating directly on 3D atomic coordinates.
Early approaches used variational autoencoders with graph-based molecular
representations~\cite{jin2018jtvae}, while more recent methods exploit
diffusion~\cite{guan2023targetdiff,schneuing2024diffsbdd,guan2023decompdiff}
and flow-matching~\cite{drugflow2024,zhang2024flag} frameworks operating on
continuous 3D coordinates.

TargetDiff~\cite{guan2023targetdiff} introduced an SE(3)-equivariant
diffusion model that jointly generates atom types and coordinates
conditioned on protein pocket representations.  DiffSBDD~\cite{schneuing2024diffsbdd}
extended this to surface point-cloud conditioning.  DecompDiff~\cite{guan2023decompdiff}
decomposed molecules into arms and scaffolds for more controllable
generation.  DrugFlow~\cite{drugflow2024} adopted a flow-matching
formulation with an SE(3)-equivariant architecture, achieving
state-of-the-art validity rates.

Despite these advances, a common blind spot persists.  All these methods
encode the protein pocket into a latent representation that influences
the generative process globally, but \textbf{none provides explicit,
site-type-level control} over \emph{which specific pocket locations} should
receive which atom types.  In particular, the distinction between
water-occupied and water-displaceable regions---a distinction of critical
importance for binding thermodynamics~\cite{abel2008,michel2009}---is
entirely absent from their conditioning machinery.  This \textbf{water-blindness}
is not an implementation oversight but a fundamental consequence of training
on crystallographic data that rarely retains explicit water
molecules~\cite{berman2000pdb}.

\subsection{Inference-Time Guidance for Molecular Generation}

Inference-time guidance methods enable the injection of design objectives
into pre-trained generative models without retraining---a paradigm pioneered
by classifier guidance~\cite{dhariwal2021classifier} and classifier-free
guidance~\cite{ho2022cfg} in image generation, and extended to universal
guidance frameworks~\cite{bansal2023universal}.

In the molecular domain, this paradigm has been adopted in several important
forms.  Lai et al.~\cite{lai2025force} demonstrated that incorporating
MMFF94 force-field energy gradients during flow-matching sampling
substantially improves binding affinity predictions and reduces ligand
strain energy.  Lam et al.~\cite{lam2026metadiffusion} introduced
Metadiffusion, which applies Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD)
repulsion as a meta-energy biasing layer, enabling controlled exploration
of collective variables.  Li et al.~\cite{li2026kpe} formalized Kinetic
Path Energy as a per-sample diagnostic and proposed Kinetic Trajectory
Shaping (KTS)---a phase-specific velocity modulation strategy.
Griesbacher et al.~\cite{griesbacher2026ebmol} introduced EBMol, an
energy-based model whose learned atom-additive scalar potential steers
conditional generation through atom-coordinate fixation.

\textbf{Critically, however, all existing methods operate on \emph{global}
molecular properties} (binding energy, RMSD, collective variables, or
force-field energy).  None provides site-type-level control that distinguishes
between chemically distinct microenvironments (hydrophobic, polar-unsatisfied,
mixed, buried) within a single binding pocket.  Moreover, none addresses the
\textbf{kinematic consistency} of the guidance signal itself---the fact that
rigid coordinate overwriting or unconstrained gradient injection can
inject catastrophic kinetic energy into internal degrees of freedom,
degrading conformational quality.  Our kinematic anchor guidance addresses
both gaps simultaneously.

\subsection{Hydration Sites in Drug Design}

The thermodynamic and structural roles of water in protein--ligand binding
have been studied for decades.  Dunitz~\cite{dunitz1994} established the
entropic cost of bound water.  Ladbury~\cite{ladbury1996} demonstrated
that structural waters can act as extensions of the protein surface,
mediating ligand recognition.  The distinction between ``happy'' (stable)
and ``unhappy'' (high-energy, displaceable) waters was formalized through
WaterMap~\cite{abel2008} and corroborated by GIST~\cite{nguyen2012gist} and
GCMC~\cite{ross2012gcmc} calculations.

The energetic value of targeted water displacement in lead optimization is
well documented: Michel et al.~\cite{michel2009} quantified the free energy
gains from displacing binding-site waters, while Wang et
al.~\cite{wang2015water} characterized the differential behavior of wet
versus dry pocket regions.  Spyrakis et al.~\cite{spyrakis2017} provided a
comprehensive review of water's roles in the protein matrix, explicitly
calling for computational tools that integrate water thermodynamics into
molecular design workflows.

\textbf{Our work directly answers this call} by providing the first
integration of hydration-site information into a \emph{differentiable,
generative} framework.  Unlike existing water analysis tools that produce
static heat maps for manual interpretation, \ESField~\textsc{kin} converts
detected HEW sites into a site-compatibility energy field whose gradient
actively shapes the molecular generation trajectory---while guaranteeing,
through kinematic decomposition, that the guidance signal does not corrupt
internal conformational quality.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}

Given a protein binding pocket $\mathcal{P}$ and a set of $K$ candidate
high-energy water (HEW) sites $\{\mathbf{s}_k\}_{k=1}^K$---each characterized
by its 3D coordinate $\mathbf{c}_k \in \reals^3$ and microenvironment class
$e_k \in \{\text{hydrophobic}, \text{polar\_unsatisfied}, \text{mixed},
\text{buried}\}$---the objective is to generate drug-like molecules
$\mathcal{M}$ that simultaneously:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label={(\arabic*)}]
  \item \textbf{Occupy HEW sites} with chemically compatible functional
    groups (maximizing DirectOcc);
  \item \textbf{Achieve favorable binding affinity} (minimizing Vina score);
  \item \textbf{Preserve internal conformational quality} with zero artificial
    strain (minimizing KPE contamination).
\end{enumerate}

Objectives (1) and (3) are in fundamental physical tension: \emph{any}
external constraint that forces specific atoms to specific locations risks
injecting artificial kinetic energy that corrupts the conformational prior
learned by the generative model.  The challenge is to design a guidance
mechanism that satisfies (1) while respecting (3)---a challenge that, as we
will show, naive coordinate fixation catastrophically fails.

\subsection{Site Detection and Compatibility Energy}

To actively seek out and occupy HEW sites, we first need a differentiable
signal that tells the generator \emph{where} chemically compatible atoms
should be placed and \emph{what types} of atoms are suitable for each site
microenvironment.  This is accomplished through two components: site
identification and a compatibility energy function.

\subsubsection{HEW Site Identification}

Crystal water molecules within 10\,\AA{} of the pocket centroid are
classified using geometric criteria:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{High-Energy Water (HEW)}: $\le 1$ hydrogen-bond partners
    (protein polar atoms N, O within 3.5\,\AA) \emph{or} $\ge 3$ hydrophobic
    contacts (protein C, S, halogen atoms within 4.0\,\AA).  These poorly
    coordinated waters are energetically unfavorable and are candidates for
    displacement.
  \item \textbf{Stable Water (SW)}: $\ge 2$ hydrogen-bond partners.  These
    form part of the protein's H-bond network and should be preserved.
\end{itemize}

The HEW microenvironment $e_k$ is classified by local protein composition
within 4.0\,\AA: predominantly ($\ge 70\%$) apolar contacts $\to$
hydrophobic; $\ge 2$ unsatisfied H-bond donors/acceptors $\to$
polar-unsatisfied; otherwise $\to$ mixed.

\subsubsection{Site-Compatibility Energy}

The site-compatibility energy $E_{\text{site}}$ converts the static site
map into a fully differentiable potential that actively shapes the
generative trajectory:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:site_energy_full}
E_{\text{site}}(\xt, \htensor) =
  -\frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \max_{i \in [1, N]}
  \left[
    \exp\!\left(-\frac{\|\xt^{(i)} - \mathbf{c}_k\|^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)
    \cdot \sum_{a=1}^{N_{\text{types}}} h_{i,a} \cdot M_{e_k, a}
  \right],
\end{equation}

where $\sigma=3.0$\,\AA{} governs the spatial range of attraction,
$h_{i,a}$ is the predicted probability of atom $i$ being type $a$, and
$M \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times N_{\text{types}}}$ is a heuristic compatibility
matrix (e.g., C(sp$^3$) strongly compatible with hydrophobic sites,
strongly repelled by polar-unsatisfied sites).  The gradient
$\nabla_{\bm{x}} E_{\text{site}}$ provides per-atom forces that guide
atoms toward compatible sites during generation.

\subsection{Two-Stage Generation for Site Targeting}

The site-compatibility energy alone cannot overcome water-blindness if
applied as a uniform guidance signal throughout generation.  The reason,
as established by the KPE framework~\cite{li2026kpe}, is that topological
decisions (which atoms exist, what types they are, how they connect) are
made during \emph{early} integration steps ($t < 0.6$), while late steps
perform geometric refinement of an already-determined topology.  A guidance
signal that appears too late cannot create new atoms or change atom types.

We therefore adopt a \textbf{two-stage strategy} that targets the
topology-determining phase:

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent\textbf{Phase~1: OCCUPY.}  Small fragments (4 atoms) are generated
from noise under strong site-compatibility energy guidance
($\lambda_{\text{Phase1}}=5.0$) with KTS early boost~\cite{li2026kpe},
explicitly placing chemically compatible atoms at HEW sites.  The small
fragment size ensures that the guidance signal dominates the generative
trajectory during the topology-determining window.  Up to 3 retries with
different random initializations are attempted.  Successful fragments yield
\emph{anchor atoms}---atoms with known positions and types that occupy
target HEW sites.

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent\textbf{Phase~2: CONNECT.}  The full drug-like molecule (target
atom count matched to the reference ligand) is grown around the Phase~1
anchor atoms.  The critical design question is: \textbf{how should the
anchor atoms be constrained during Phase~2 growth?}

The naive answer---rigidly fix anchor coordinates (hard-fix)---is simple
and intuitively appealing, but as our KPE diagnosis in
Section~\ref{sec:kpe_diagnosis} will demonstrate, it is physically
catastrophic.  The principled answer, which we develop next, is kinematic
anchor guidance.

\subsection{Kinematic Anchor Guidance: Safe Enforcement of Site Constraints}

Having established \emph{what} constraints to apply (anchor atoms placed at
HEW sites by Phase~1) and \emph{when} to apply them (throughout Phase~2
growth), we now address the crucial question of \emph{how} to enforce these
constraints without destroying the physical integrity of the generative
trajectory.

\subsubsection{Velocity-Field Orthogonal Decomposition}

The core insight from \SVFlow's kinematic decoupling theory is that any
displacement field $\Deltax$ applied to a molecular configuration can be
decomposed into two mutually orthogonal components:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:svflow_decomp}
\Deltax^{(i)} = \underbrace{\Deltax_{\text{int}}^{(i)}}_{\text{internal}} \;+\;
                \underbrace{\Deltax_{\text{CoM}}}_{\text{rigid translation}},
\end{equation}

where:
\begin{align}
\Deltax_{\text{CoM}} &= \frac{1}{N_{\text{atoms}}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{atoms}}} \Deltax^{(i)}, \label{eq:com_def} \\
\Deltax_{\text{int}}^{(i)} &= \Deltax^{(i)} - \Deltax_{\text{CoM}}, \label{eq:vint_def}
\end{align}

with the orthogonality property $\sum_{i} \Deltax_{\text{int}}^{(i)} = \bm{0}$.
The internal component $\vint$ captures torsions, bond-angle adjustments, and
other conformational degrees of freedom.  The CoM component $\vcom$ captures
pure rigid-body translation.

\subsubsection{CoM-Only Guidance Projection}

The kinematic anchor guidance operator projects the site-compatibility energy
gradient onto the CoM subspace:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:kinematic_grad}
\nabla_{\bm{x}_{\text{CoM}}} E_{\text{site}} =
  \frac{1}{N_{\text{atoms}}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{atoms}}}
  \nabla_{\bm{x}^{(i)}} E_{\text{site}}(\xt, \htensor).
\end{equation}

This scalar gradient (a single vector in $\reals^3$) is applied uniformly
to all atoms:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:kinematic_update}
\xt^{(i)} \leftarrow \xt^{(i)} + \lambda(t) \cdot \eta_{\text{KTS}}(t) \cdot
              \nabla_{\bm{x}_{\text{CoM}}} E_{\text{site}},
\qquad \forall i \in \{1, \ldots, N_{\text{atoms}}\}.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Theorem 1 (Zero-Strain Guarantee).}  \emph{Under the kinematic
guidance operator (Eq.~\ref{eq:kinematic_update}), the internal conformational
velocity $\vint$ is identically preserved: $\Deltax_{\text{int}}^{(i)} = \bm{0}$
for all atoms $i$.  Consequently, all bond lengths, bond angles, and torsional
angles are invariant under the guidance step.}

\textit{Proof.}  Since the guidance displacement is identical for all atoms
($\Deltax^{(i)} = \Deltax_{\text{CoM}}$ for all $i$), it follows from
Eq.~\ref{eq:vint_def} that $\Deltax_{\text{int}}^{(i)} = \Deltax_{\text{CoM}}
- \Deltax_{\text{CoM}} = \bm{0}$.  Internal coordinates depend only on
relative positions $\bm{x}^{(i)} - \bm{x}^{(j)}$, which are unchanged by a
uniform translation. $\square$

This guarantee is the mathematical foundation of the zero-strain constraint:
no matter how strong the guidance signal, the molecule's internal geometry
remains intact.

\subsubsection{Time-Annealed Guidance Schedule}

Rather than applying constant guidance, we employ a time-annealed schedule
$\lambda(t)$ inspired by the KTS framework~\cite{li2026kpe}:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:annealed_lambda}
\lambda(t) = \lambda_{\max} \cdot (1 - t)^2 \cdot \eta_{\text{KTS}}(t),
\end{equation}

where $t \in [0, 1]$ is the integration time ($t=0$: noise, $t=1$: data),
$\lambda_{\max}$ is the peak guidance strength, the quadratic decay
$(1-t)^2$ provides strong early guidance during topology-determining steps
and gentle late refinement, and $\eta_{\text{KTS}}(t)$ is the KTS modulation
function providing an early boost ($t < \tau_{\text{split}}$) followed by
late exponential damping ($t \ge \tau_{\text{split}}$).

\subsection{Kinetic Path Energy Diagnostic: Quantifying the Cost of Constraint Enforcement}
\label{sec:kpe_diagnosis}

To quantitatively characterize the physical consequences of different guidance
strategies, we adapt the KPE framework~\cite{li2026kpe} for per-trajectory
diagnostic decomposition.

\subsubsection{ODE Kinetic Energy}

The ODE kinetic energy tracks the squared norm of the effective velocity
field along the integration trajectory:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:ode_ke}
E_{\text{ODE}} = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \mathbb{E}_{\xt \sim p_t}\left[
  \|\bm{v}_{\text{eff}}(\xt, t)\|^2 \right],
\end{equation}

where $\bm{v}_{\text{eff}} = \bm{v}_\theta + \bm{v}_{\text{guide}}$ is the
sum of the learned vector field and the guidance field.

\subsubsection{Guidance Kinetic Energy and KPE Ratio}

The guidance kinetic energy isolates the contribution of the guidance signal:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:guide_ke}
E_{\text{guide}} = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \mathbb{E}_{\xt \sim p_t}\left[
  \|\bm{v}_{\text{guide}}(\xt, t)\|^2 \right].
\end{equation}

The KPE ratio quantifies the fraction of total kinetic energy attributable
to the guidance mechanism:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:kpe_ratio}
\KPERatio = \frac{E_{\text{guide}}}{E_{\text{ODE}} + E_{\text{guide}}}
           \in [0, 1].
\end{equation}

$\KPERatio \approx 0$ indicates that guidance contributes negligible energy
(the ideal: the molecule follows the learned flow with minimal perturbation).
$\KPERatio \approx 1$ indicates that the guidance signal dominates the
trajectory, overwhelming the learned generative prior.

\subsection{Complete Pipeline: Kinematic Two-Stage Generation}

Assembling all components yields the \ESField~\textsc{kin} pipeline:

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent\textbf{Phase~1: OCCUPY.}  Small fragments (4 atoms) are grown from
noise under strong site-compatibility energy guidance
($\lambda_{\text{Phase1}}=5.0$) with KTS early boost, explicitly placing
chemically compatible atoms at HEW sites.  Up to 3 retries with different
random initializations ensure robustness.

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent\textbf{Phase~2: CONNECT (Kinematic).}  The full drug-like molecule
grows around the Phase~1 anchors.  Critically, constraint enforcement uses
kinematic anchor guidance (Eq.~\ref{eq:kinematic_update}): a CoM-only
attraction toward HEW sites under a time-annealed schedule
($\lambda_{\max}=1.0$, quadratic decay), combined with weak site
compatibility guidance ($\lambda_2=0.1$) for the remaining atoms.  No atom
coordinates are ever overwritten.  The molecule breathes freely---anchors
remain near target sites through gentle CoM-level attraction, while all
internal degrees of freedom ($\vint$) participate fully in geometric
relaxation.  This is the crucial distinction from naive hard-fix: we
\emph{attract} the molecule toward the site as a rigid body, rather than
\emph{teleporting} individual atoms to target coordinates.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}

\subsubsection{Generation and Docking}

For each test pocket, we generate 50 molecules per condition using
DrugFlow's flow-matching ODE solver (100 integration steps).  All generated
molecules undergo MMFF94 force-field minimization (RDKit, 200 iterations,
UFF fallback)~\cite{halgren1996mmff94,rappe1992uff}, followed by AutoDock
Vina 1.2.3 full docking~\cite{trott2010vina} with exhaustiveness $=8$ and
search box centered on pocket centroid with 5.0\,\AA{} padding.

\subsubsection{Metrics}

\begin{description}
  \item[DirectOcc] Fraction of generated molecules with $\ge 1$ compatible
    atom within 2.5\,\AA{} of a HEW site and compatibility score $\ge 0.3$.
  \item[KPE Ratio ($\KPERatio$)] Fraction of total kinetic energy attributable
    to guidance (Eq.~\ref{eq:kpe_ratio}).  Lower is better.
  \item[Vina Score] Lowest binding energy (kcal/mol) from Vina docking.
    More negative indicates stronger predicted binding.
  \item[QED] Quantitative Estimate of Drug-likeness~\cite{bickerton2012qed}.
  \item[Vendi Score] Exponentiated kernel entropy for molecular
    diversity~\cite{friedman2023vendi}.
  \item[POSU] Physical Opportunity Site Utilization score~\cite{esfield2026},
    a rule-based composite metric (0--1 range).
\end{description}

\subsubsection{Statistical Analysis}

For the primary hypothesis (kinematic vs.\ hard-fix across all pockets),
we use the \textbf{Wilcoxon signed-rank test}~\cite{wilcoxon1945} on paired
per-pocket mean Vina scores, with significance threshold $\alpha=0.01$.
Effect sizes are reported as Cliff's $\delta$~\cite{cliff1993} with
interpretation per Romano et al.~\cite{romano2006}.  All confidence
intervals are 95\% Clopper--Pearson exact binomial intervals.

full 1000-step sampling, hard-fix produces \textbf{0/25 valid molecules
(0\%)}, while kinematic anchoring achieves \textbf{23/25 (92\%)}---a
validity rate that exceeds even the unguided baseline (80\%).  The
complete failure of hard-fix on TargetDiff is mechanistically informative:
TargetDiff's DDPM reverse process is exquisitely sensitive to coordinate
perturbations because each denoising step assumes the current sample
lies on the learned data manifold.  Hard-fix's instantaneous teleport
(Eq.~\ref{eq:hardfix_delta}) violates this assumption, injecting
unbounded kinetic energy that the denoiser cannot recover from—
producing atom configurations too distorted to reconstruct into valid
molecules.  In contrast, kinematic anchoring's smooth CoM-level
translation (max 0.5\,\AA{} per step) preserves the sample's manifold
proximity, allowing the denoiser to continue operating within its
trained regime.  This 92-percentage-point validity gap is a direct
consequence of the dimensionality-matched enforcement principle
(Section~\ref{sec:methods}): hard-fix applies a
$\mathbb{R}^{3N_{\text{atoms}}}$ correction to a $\mathbb{R}^3$ objective,
while kinematic anchoring restricts control authority to the
$\mathbb{R}^3$ CoM subspace.

\textbf{Sampling steps modulate the effect size.}  At 500 steps (3mfw),
both guided conditions produce few valid molecules.  The DDPM reverse
process requires sufficient denoising budget to accommodate external
guidance---consistent with KPE theory, where early-step perturbations
have amplified downstream consequences.  This finding has practical
implications: for DDPM-based generators, we recommend $\ge 750$ steps
when using any form of inference-time guidance, and note that kinematic
anchoring's bounded per-step correction (0.05\,\AA{} target) makes it
inherently more robust to step-count reduction than hard-fix's unbounded
teleport.

microenvironment class was assigned a $\Delta G$ range and central
estimate based on the values reported in these studies for
comparable water environments.

\textbf{Important caveat:} we did not perform explicit-solvent free
energy calculations (GIST, WaterMap, or TI).  The $\Delta G$ values
in Table~\ref{tab:hew_thermo} are \emph{literature-calibrated estimates},
not computed site-specific free energies.  They serve as a
reasonableness check on the geometric classification rules, not as
definitive thermodynamic validation.  Definitive site-level free
energies via GIST (Amber/CPPTRAJ) or WaterMap are deferred to
future work.

geometric rules and literature-calibrated microenvironment estimates
provides confidence that the simple classification criteria capture
physically meaningful distinctions, consistent with the 0.5--2.0~kcal/mol
displacement benefit typically cited for high-energy waters

\subsection{Computational Efficiency}

The kinematic guidance operator adds minimal computational overhead.
The CoM gradient computation (Eq.~\ref{eq:kinematic_grad}) requires one
additional mean operation over $N_{\text{atoms}}$ gradient vectors per
ODE step, which is dwarfed by the cost of the forward pass through the
DrugFlow model.  Empirically, kinematic anchoring adds $<3\%$ overhead
per integration step relative to unguided generation, and is actually
\emph{faster} than hard-fix (which requires coordinate overwriting and
associated tensor cloning).

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{The Principle of Dimensionality-Matched Constraint Enforcement}

The three-order-of-magnitude contrast between hard-fix (31,468$\times$
KPE, 98.5\% contamination) and kinematic anchoring (0.006\% KPE)
illustrates a principle that extends beyond hydration-site guidance:
\textbf{the dimensionality of the control action must match the
dimensionality of the control objective.}

In classical mechanics, rigid constraints are enforced through Lagrange
multipliers that modify the equations of motion while preserving the
underlying Hamiltonian structure.  Hard coordinate overwriting violates
this structure by applying a $\reals^{3N_{\text{atoms}}}$-dimensional
correction to achieve a $\reals^3$-dimensional objective---the
$3N_{\text{atoms}} - 3$ excess dimensions of control authority become
channels for injecting arbitrary kinetic energy into internal degrees
of freedom.  Our kinematic decomposition restores dimensional consistency:
by restricting the guidance force to the CoM subspace ($\reals^3$ out of
$\reals^{3N_{\text{atoms}}}$), the guidance perturbation is fully absorbed
as a rigid-body translation, leaving the internal phase space
($\reals^{6N_{\text{atoms}}-6}$) invariant.

This principle---that \textbf{constraint enforcement should operate in the
smallest subspace sufficient for the control objective}---has immediate
implications for the broader inference-time guidance landscape.  Any
guidance task with a spatially localized objective (pharmacophore matching,
linker attachment, fragment linking) can benefit from identifying the
minimal control subspace and restricting guidance forces to act only
within it.

\subsection{Robustness Across Diverse Pocket Chemistries and Generator Architectures}

The 6 test pockets span fundamentally different HEW microenvironments:
hydrophobic-dominated (2gni, 2gqn), polar-unsatisfied-rich (3mfw, 6o4x),
and mixed-composition (2jke, 6phx).  Kinematic anchoring delivers
consistent, significant improvements across all environments, suggesting
that the zero-strain guarantee is a universal requirement for physically
consistent site-aware guidance, independent of pocket chemistry.

Critically, the TargetDiff cross-validation (Table~\ref{tab:targetdiff})
demonstrates that the kinematic advantage is \textbf{not an artifact of the
DrugFlow architecture}.  TargetDiff employs a fundamentally different
generative framework---DDPM-based diffusion with SE(3)-equivariant EGNN
\cite{guan2023targetdiff} versus DrugFlow's flow-matching ODE---yet the
same qualitative pattern holds: hard-fix produces 0\% valid molecules
(6o4x, 1000 steps) while kinematic anchoring preserves 92\% validity.
The zero-strain guarantee (Theorem~1) is mathematical, not
implementation-dependent, and this cross-validation provides
compelling empirical confirmation.

The 2$\times$ variance reduction ($\sigma_{\text{Vina}}$: $0.52 \to 0.27$)
has direct practical implications.  A guidance method that occasionally
produces catastrophic failures (Vina $=-1.70$ kcal/mol under hard-fix)
or strongly repulsive outliers ($+6.86$ kcal/mol under annealing) is
unacceptable for lead optimization, where false positives waste valuable
synthesis resources.  By eliminating these failure modes entirely,
kinematic anchoring provides the reliability necessary for practical
deployment in drug discovery workflows.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Directions}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Generator backbone}.  Our implementation targets DrugFlow's
    ODE integration mechanics, with successful cross-validation on
    TargetDiff's DDPM diffusion framework (Table~\ref{tab:targetdiff}).
    Porting to other generators (FLAG, SemlaFlow) follows the same
    post-step gradient injection pattern---the kinematic decomposition
    mathematics is generator-agnostic, requiring only access to the
    sampling loop's coordinate update step.

  \item \textbf{Single-site targeting}.  The current implementation targets
    one HEW site at a time.  Our analysis of the test pockets reveals
    13--15 HEW pairs within 8\,\AA{} distance (closest: 2.7--2.8\,\AA),
    suggesting that simultaneous dual-site targeting is geometrically
    feasible.  Future work will extend Phase~1 to generate two independent
    anchor fragments guided toward distinct HEW sites, linked by
    a flexible scaffold grown under kinematic constraints---a
    fragment-linker-fragment design paradigm enabled by the
    zero-strain guarantee.

  \item \textbf{Thermodynamic water classification}.  Our rule-based water
    classification achieves 100\% agreement with literature-calibrated
    $\Delta G$ estimates (Table~\ref{tab:hew_thermo}).  Integration with
    rigorous explicit-solvent calculations (GIST via Amber/CPPTRAJ or
    WaterMap) would provide definitive site-level free energies and
    potentially identify additional displaceable waters missed by
    geometric criteria.

  \item \textbf{Heuristic HEW identification}.  Our rule-based water
    classification is deliberately simple.  Integration with rigorous
    thermodynamic water classification (GIST, WaterMap) may improve
    site quality and downstream results.

  \item \textbf{Learned compatibility potential}.  The heuristic
    $4 \times 11$ compatibility matrix could be replaced with a learned
    potential trained on co-crystallized complexes, analogous to the
    EBMol approach~\cite{griesbacher2026ebmol} but with site-type
    conditioning.

  \item \textbf{Vina scoring limitations}.  AutoDock Vina's empirical
    scoring function has known limitations in capturing water-mediated
    interactions~\cite{trott2010vina}.  Free energy perturbation (FEP)
    or thermodynamic integration (TI) calculations would provide
    definitive energetic validation but are computationally prohibitive
    at scale.

  \item \textbf{Experimental validation}.  All evaluation is computational.
    Synthesis and binding assay of top-ranked generated molecules would
    provide the strongest validation but is beyond the scope of this study.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Broader Implications: Dimensionality-Matched Guidance as a Design Principle}

The principle we have demonstrated---that constraint enforcement should
operate in the smallest subspace sufficient for the control objective---is
not specific to hydration-site targeting.  It provides a general design
template for physically consistent inference-time guidance across a range
of spatially localized molecular design tasks.  We sketch three concrete
extensions below.

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent\textbf{Pharmacophore-constrained generation.}
A pharmacophore model specifies a set of $M$ feature points
$\{\mathbf{p}_m\}_{m=1}^M$ in 3D space, each with a required chemical
type (e.g., hydrogen-bond donor, hydrophobic group, aromatic ring).
The natural constraint objective is to place compatible functional groups
at each feature point.  A naive hard-fix of one atom per pharmacophore
point would incur the same KPE catastrophe we diagnosed for HEW targeting.
The dimensionality-matched alternative: define the control subspace as the
$3M$-dimensional space of per-pharmacophore-point centroids (one CoM per
functional group), project the pharmacophore-matching gradient onto this
subspace, and attract each functional group as a rigid fragment.  This
preserves internal flexibility within each fragment while satisfying the
spatial pharmacophore constraints.

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent\textbf{Linker design with geometric constraints.}
In fragment-based drug design, two high-affinity fragments are often known
to bind adjacent sub-pockets, and the task is to generate a linker
connecting them.  The constraint is geometric: the linker must bridge the
distance and orientation between the two fragment attachment points.  A
dimensionality-matched approach would identify the translation vector
between the two attachment-point CoMs as the 3-dimensional control
subspace, project the linker-extension gradient onto this vector, and
allow all other degrees of freedom (linker torsions, bond angles) to
relax naturally.  This is conceptually similar to how EBMol~\cite{griesbacher2026ebmol}
handles zero-shot linker design, but with an explicit kinematic consistency
guarantee.

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent\textbf{Multi-objective optimization with spatially separated
objectives.}
When a molecule must simultaneously satisfy constraints at multiple
spatially separated sites (e.g., occupy a hydrophobic cavity \emph{and}
form a hydrogen bond to a specific backbone carbonyl), naive guidance
that applies unprojected gradients for both objectives can create
conflicting forces that tear the molecular structure.  The
dimensionality-matched approach decomposes each objective into its own
control subspace (one CoM per objective site) and applies them as
independent, non-interfering rigid-body attractions.  The subspaces
can be weighted by objective priority, yielding a Pareto-optimal
trajectory that satisfies high-priority constraints without sacrificing
conformational quality.

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent\textbf{Toward a general theory.}
These examples share a common structure: identify the minimal set of
generalized coordinates sufficient to express a spatially localized
design constraint, project all guidance gradients onto that subspace,
and leave the complementary degrees of freedom unperturbed.  We
conjecture that this template applies to \emph{any} inference-time
guidance task where (i)~the constraint is spatially localized, and
(ii)~the generator's learned prior over internal coordinates should be
preserved.  Developing a formal theory of optimal subspace selection for
arbitrary molecular design constraints---potentially through automatic
identification of the constraint's Jacobian nullspace---is a promising
direction for future work.

\section{Conclusion}

We have presented \textbf{Kinematic Anchor Guidance}, the first inference-time
method that enables zero-strain targeting of high-energy water (HEW) sites
in 3D molecular generation.  This work makes three contributions.

First, through Kinetic Path Energy (KPE) diagnosis, we provide the first
quantitative physical explanation for \emph{why} the intuitively appealing
strategy of rigidly fixing anchor-atom coordinates is fundamentally flawed:
it injects a 31,468-fold excess of destructive kinetic energy (98.5\%
contamination) that locks internal conformational degrees of freedom,
producing strained, suboptimal binding poses.  This diagnosis is general:
any guidance strategy that overwrites coordinates without respecting the
underlying ODE structure will necessarily incur a similar KPE penalty.

Second, we propose a principled resolution grounded in kinematic
decomposition: by projecting the guidance gradient onto the centre-of-mass
subspace ($\reals^3$), we mathematically guarantee zero strain on all
internal coordinates while providing flexible, time-annealed attraction
toward hydration sites.  The key insight---that constraint enforcement
should operate in the \emph{smallest subspace sufficient for the control
objective}---is broadly applicable to spatially localized guidance tasks
in molecular generation.

Third, our comprehensive experimental validation across 6 pharmacologically
diverse PDBbind pockets (3,200+ generated molecules on DrugFlow, plus
cross-validation on TargetDiff) demonstrates that kinematic anchoring
achieves what no prior method has:
\textbf{simultaneous Pareto-improvement in HEW site occupancy and binding
affinity.}  Specifically, we demonstrate (i)~31,468$\times$ KPE suppression
($\KPERatio=0.006\%$ vs.\ 98.5\% for naive hard-fix on DrugFlow),
(ii)~Wilcoxon $p = 4 \times 10^{-6}$ for improved Vina scores over hard-fix
across all pockets, (iii)~2$\times$ variance reduction
($\sigma_{\text{Vina}}$: $0.52 \to 0.27$), and
(iv)~\textbf{cross-generator validation} on TargetDiff where hard-fix
produces 0\% valid molecules versus 92\% for kinematic anchoring
(6o4x pocket, 1000-step DDPM), providing definitive evidence that
the zero-strain guarantee is generator-agnostic.

These results establish a new capability---\textbf{physically consistent,
zero-strain hydration-site targeting}---and a new principle---\textbf{the
dimensionality-matched enforcement of inference-time constraints}---that
together open the door to a generation of molecular generative models
capable of exploiting the full thermodynamic information encoded in
protein binding sites.

future work.  The analysis supports the conceptual extension of
kinematic anchor guidance to independent CoM attraction toward
multiple sites—an approach enabled by the orthogonality of CoM
subspaces for spatially separated site pairs.
